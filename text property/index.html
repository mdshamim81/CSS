Iron Man

Iron Man is a 2008 American superhero film based on the Marvel Comics character of the same name. Produced by Marvel Studios and distributed by Paramount Pictures, it is the first film in the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU). Directed by Jon Favreau from a screenplay by the writing teams of Mark Fergus & Hawk Ostby and Art Marcum & Matt Holloway, the film stars Robert Downey Jr. as Tony Stark Iron Man alongside Terrence Howard, Jeff Bridges, Gwyneth Paltrow, Leslie Bibb, and Shaun Toub. In the film, world-famous industrialist and master engineer Stark builds a mechanized suit of armor to escape from captivity by a terrorist group, and then uses it to become the superhero Iron Man.

Publication History

Premiere

The Creators: He was developed by writer-editor Stan Lee, screenwriter Larry Lieber, artist Don Heck, and legendary character designer Jack Kirby. The First Suit: In his first appearance, the armor wasn't red and gold; it was a bulky, grey suit made of scrap metal. It was painted gold in the very next issue (Tales of Suspense #40) to look less frightening to the public. Solo Debut: After sharing Tales of Suspense with Captain America for years, Iron Man finally got his own standalone series, "The Invincible Iron Man #1", in May 1968. A Founding Member: Just months after his debut, he became a founding member of the Avengers in The Avengers #1 (September 1963).

Solo series

The Launch (1968): His first independent title, The Invincible Iron Man, debuted in May 1968. This followed a "one-shot" comic called Iron Man and Sub-Mariner that served as a bridge between his old anthology stories and his new solo run. The Iconic "Volume 1": This original series ran for 332 issues (until 1996). This era defined the character, introducing his struggle with alcoholism in the famous "Demon in a Bottle" arc and the high-tech corporate warfare of "Stark Wars" (later known as Armor Wars).

Fictional Character biography

The fictional biography of Anthony "Tony" Stark, better known as Iron Man, is a complex journey of redemption that differs significantly between the original Marvel Comics (Earth-616) and the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU).
